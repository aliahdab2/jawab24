## Every Comment Is a Buying Signal

When someone comments "How much?" on your product post, that's not a casual question. That person is ready to buy. They've seen your product, they're interested, and they're asking for the one piece of information they need to make a decision.

But here's what usually happens: by the time you see the comment, reply, wait for them to check their DMs, and send the price — they've already found it somewhere else. Or they've simply lost interest.

The gap between a comment and a sale is measured in minutes, not hours. Research shows that responding within 1 minute produces **391% more conversions** than responding after 30 minutes. And the average business takes over 12 hours to respond to a social media message.

That's not a customer service problem. That's a sales problem.

## The Comment-to-Sale Pipeline

Every product comment follows the same path — whether you manage it or not:

**Stage 1: The Comment.** Someone writes "Price?" or "Is this available?" or "What sizes do you have?" on your post. This is the highest-intent moment. They're actively shopping.

**Stage 2: The Reply.** You respond in the comments or send a DM with the answer. If this takes more than a few minutes, you've likely lost them. If you never respond, you've definitely lost them.

**Stage 3: The Conversation.** The customer has follow-up questions — "Do you ship to Riyadh?", "What about the blue one?", "Can I pay on delivery?" Each unanswered question is a dropped sale.

**Stage 4: The Close.** The customer has all the information and is ready to order. Someone needs to take the order, confirm the details, and close the sale.

Most businesses fail at Stage 2. They see the comment hours later, send a generic reply, and wonder why their social media "doesn't convert."

## Why a Human Can't Do This at Scale

If you get 10 comments a day, you can handle it manually. But what happens when you run an ad that gets 200 comments? Or when you post a product reel that goes viral?

The math doesn't work:

- Each comment needs a personalized response (not "DM us for price" — that kills conversions)
- Follow-up questions need accurate answers about your specific products, prices, and availability
- Customers comment at all hours — 11 PM on a Friday, 6 AM on a holiday, during your lunch break
- Arabic-speaking customers might write in Egyptian dialect, Gulf dialect, or Levantine — and expect a natural response in their dialect

A single employee handling social media can realistically manage 30-40 meaningful conversations per day. An AI sales agent handles thousands — simultaneously, instantly, in any dialect, at any hour.

## What an AI Sales Agent Does Differently

An AI sales agent isn't a chatbot that says "Thanks for your message! A team member will get back to you soon." That's an auto-responder — and it kills sales because the customer knows nobody is actually there.

An AI sales agent does what your best salesperson does:

**Understands the question.** When someone comments "بكم الأحمر؟" (How much is the red one?), the agent knows they're asking about the red variant of the product in the post — not any random red product in your catalog.

**Answers with real data.** The agent pulls the actual price from your product catalog. No guessing, no hallucinating a wrong price. If the red variant is 149 SAR, it says 149 SAR.

**Handles follow-ups.** "And the blue one?" — The agent knows "the blue one" refers to the same product, different color. It checks the catalog and responds with the correct price for the blue variant.

**Speaks naturally.** If the customer writes in Egyptian Arabic, the agent responds in Egyptian Arabic. If they switch to English mid-conversation, the agent switches too. No robotic, formal-Arabic responses that feel like a Google Translate output.

**Handles voice messages.** In Arabic-speaking markets, many customers prefer sending voice messages over typing. An AI sales agent transcribes the audio, understands the request, and responds — instead of ignoring the message or sending "Sorry, we can't read voice messages."

**Knows when to escalate.** Complex complaints, custom orders, or situations that need a human touch — the agent flags these for your team instead of trying to handle everything.
